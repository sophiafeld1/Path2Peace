# EMDR Therapy (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing)

## Definition
A therapeutic approach designed to help clients process and heal from traumatic experiences and distressing memories. Developed in the late 1980s, focuses on treating conditions like PTSD.

## How It Works
Therapists guide clients through bilateral eye movements or rhythmic stimulation (taps or sounds) while recalling distressing memories. This helps the brain reprocess traumatic memories, reducing their emotional intensity.

## The Eight Phases
1. Client preparation and history gathering
2. Desensitization of specific memories
3. Processing of traumatic content
4. Integration of new insights into daily life

## Neurological Basis
Leverages the brain's natural recovery mechanisms involving three key areas:
- Amygdala (alarm response)
- Hippocampus (memory and safety learning)
- Prefrontal cortex (behavior and emotion regulation)

## Outcomes
Many clients find EMDR an effective and relatively rapid method for addressing deep-rooted emotional issues stemming from traumatic events.
